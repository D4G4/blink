I built an app because every 20-20-20 rule app I tried, I ended up disabling within a week.

The 20-20-20 rule is simple: every 20 minutes, look 20 feet away for 20 seconds. Optometrists have recommended it since the 90s. The problem isn't the rule — it's the apps.

They interrupt you mid-thought. They fire a popup while you're in a meeting. They nag you when you already walked away 2 minutes ago. They don't care that you're deep in a coding session and interrupting now will cost you 23 minutes to get back into flow.

So I built Blink.

**What makes it different:**

It watches how you work (not what you work on). Keystroke rhythm, app switching patterns, mouse behavior — it builds a real-time picture of whether you're in flow or just browsing.

- In flow? It extends the timer and waits for a natural pause in your typing before prompting. No mid-thought interruptions.
- Walked away from your desk? It notices and counts that as a break. Timer resets silently.
- In a meeting? Timer pauses automatically.
- Watching a video? Timer resets — you're already looking at a distance (sort of).
- Waiting for an AI agent to respond? It knows you're still there. Timer keeps running.

When it's time for a break, you get a gentle 3-second heads-up in the corner, then a clean 20-second screen takeover. Hit Esc to skip, arrow key to extend. No guilt trips, no streaks, no gamification.

The whole point: an app that's smart enough that you forget it's running, until the exact right moment.

**It's free. It's open source. macOS only (for now).**
